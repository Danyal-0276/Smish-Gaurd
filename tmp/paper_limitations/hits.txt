Despite the promising capabilities of transformer models, current research in FND has several limitations that affect both theoretical understanding and practical deployment. Various studies have observed that these models are treated as black boxes: evaluating their performance without understanding which architectural components contribute the most significantly to their effectiveness [ 8]. Stating these limits prevents the strong in-domain results from being interpreted as universal fact-checking performance. 8.1. Single-seed and statistical uncertainty The reported experiment uses one fixed seed. This design makes the run reproducible but does not quantify variation across random initializations ( 24, 32). Future work should repeat every model and feature setting across several seeds and report the mean, standard deviation, confidence interval, and rank stability. The next evaluation should also retain paired claim-level predictions so that McNemar tests can compare models without reconstructing invalid statistics from aggregate confusion matrices (35). 8.2. Calibration and subgroup evaluation The study does not evaluate probability calibration or subgroup performance. Interface scores must therefore not be interpreted as calibrated probabilities of truth ( 22, 23). Future experiments should report reliability diagrams, expected calibration error, Brier score, and calibration before and after temperature scaling ( 36, 37). They should also report performance across speakers, topics, part

PAGE 21: training distribution, and compression does not guarantee equal robustness (38). A struc- tured benchmark design similar to WILDS would help separate temporal, geographic, topical, and source shifts instead of reporting one combined external score (39). 8.4. Evidence and application limits The system does not retrieve primary sources, model multimodal evidence, or verify numerical claims against external databases. Affective tag serialization also provides a simple auxiliary representation rather than learned multimodal fusion. Future work should combine claim classification with evidence retrieval and ranking, following evidence-aware and benchmark-based fact-checking research ( 21, 28, 29). It should then evaluate how journalists and researchers use the output rather than assuming that interface availability proves practical benefit (33). Until then, outputs should support human review rather than replace it (1,2). 8.5. Future research roadmap Future work should proceed in five stages. First, repeated multi-seed experiments should quantify optimization uncertainty and test whether encoder rankings remain stable (24, 32). Second, claim-level prediction records should support McNemar tests, bootstrap confidence intervals, and transparent error comparisons (35).
